Questions before prompts.

The better the questions, the less the creator has to surrender to whatever the tool happens to produce first.

Song

What does the song know that ordinary speech does not?

Where does the chorus lift, break, turn, confess or invite?

What should the viewer understand by feeling, not explanation?

World

Where does this song happen: a room, road, beach, city, memory, dream, ritual, future or ordinary day?

What objects carry meaning without needing a speech?

What colour would the song refuse?

Story Mode

Should the video show the lyrics directly, deepen them through a small story, or move more symbolically?

What would a funny version reveal?

What would a tender version protect?

Audience

Who is this clip for: fans, first listeners, collaborators, festival programmers, local community, a private circle, or the creator's future self?

What should they be able to do next?

What should they not be pushed into?

Boundaries

Whose image, voice, story, place, culture or memory could be affected?

What should stay abstract, private, off-camera or consent-checked?

What would make the creator proud after the novelty wears off?

Release

Is this a teaser, lyric clip, festival submission, live backdrop, vertical short, full music video or collaborator pitch?

What version is safe enough to share now?

What version still needs human review?

Turn the answers into a file.

The builders turn these questions into Markdown, which is just plain text with headings. Plain text is easy for humans, agents and future tools to read.
